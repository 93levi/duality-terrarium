Problem Space

Students lack the subconscious infrastructure for character acquisition.

Japanese learners around the world are often faced with a fundamental issue that learners of many other languages never encounter; learning to recognise thousands of complex Kanji characters. Most university Japanese programs or language schools will use a textbook series like Genki and expect students to learn over 20 new characters a week for each lesson with very little direct guidance on how to do so.

For Japanese children entering elementary school this approach works well as native born students have grown up being constantly exposed to Kanji all around them, leading to the development of a deep subconscious infrastructure for character pattern recognition. Foreigners have no such infrastructure and are often thrown in the deep end and expected to learn through rote memorisation, this method is incredibly ineffective as an untrained brain has no mechanism to grasp these complex patterns and relationships, leading to an extremely steep learning curve where kanji is just treated by the brain as random noise.

This poses the question, what if there was a way to fast track the mental adoption of this subconscious infrastructure by creating a visual representation of what is going on inside the brain of someone who already recognises these complex patterns?

Core Concept

An interactive 3D environment that intuitively strengthens pattern recognition.

Duality Terrarium attempts to answer this question with a first of its kind approach. An in depth, 3D environment that allows users to interact with Kanji through a truly novel and deeply tactile interface, while simultaneously breaking each character apart into its components with real time data like component meanings and related characters. Duality Terrarium provides a particularly unique tool for beginner to intermediate learners who are making the jump from romanisation and Kana, to Kanji, providing a means to accelerate their progress towards developing a robust mental framework for character acquisition.

The dictionary feature acts as an open world sandbox for Kanji, allowing learners to enter over 6000 unique characters, interact with them in real time and break them apart, helping to intuitively strengthen their mental map of how different components interact. This feature also includes a fully integrated glossary of Kanji that share any given component, allowing inquisitive learners to go down a potentially endless rabbit hole of new characters as they continue to strengthen their subconscious infrastructure.

The textbook feature is fully integrated with the Genki and Minna No Nihongo textbooks, giving students who are taking introductory Japanese classes a real edge right at the beginning when picking up the skill of Kanji acquisition is most crucial.

Design Philosophy

An experience that evokes curiosity and invites learners to rabbit-hole deeply.

For the better part of a decade, education technology has been heavily dominated by a particular kind of user experience: The gamification, dopamine addiction model, often characterized by flat, corporate art styles and ultra-minimalism. Duality Terrarium rejects this approach, in favour of retro-futurism as its design philosophy.

The prevalent digital design language of the 2000s treated technology as something that can integrate into the natural world and enrich our lives, evoking a feeling of Millennial Optimism; the idea that technological advancements are something to look forward to.

A terrarium is a man made technology that preserves and nurtures the beauty of the natural world, evoking wonder and curiosity in the spectator. With this sentiment in mind, Duality Terrarium’s design language was born.

An intricate choreography of translucent glass windows, continuously interacting with lush green moss and the colossal Kanji mega structures that fly beneath their luminescent surface. The user is invited to explore the environment as elements expand and transform with every click, allowing them to learn intuitively without the need for any explicit lessons.

LAYER 1: DATA SOURCE

Duality Terrarium's foundation and single source of truth is the KanjiVG dataset. Created in 2009 and published on GitHub under free use, this data set contains the pure stroke geometry for 6703 individual Kanji characters and has become an industry standard, used by official dictionary websites and apps like Duolingo. Every character has an entry that contains an SVG path file that follows the physical geometry of a given stroke, in the form of a 2D vector. Each stroke within a given character is also grouped together into nested components, allowing the complete character to be broken apart and act as its own decomposition tree. A supplementary dataset called KANJIDIC2 was also used for displaying additional metadata like a character's English meaning or JLPT level, and integrated with KanjiVG using a given character's raw character glyph as its universal unique identifier.

LAYER 2: CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM

A custom built classification system was used to split each KanjiVG entry from being a static sculpture to two named halves that could become their own independently addressable object that also carries KANJIDIC2 meta data. The system classifies every KanjiVG entry into one of three groups based on its own nested component data. Characters made up of only a single component are tagged as group one, so that they can't be split apart at all. Group two characters make up over 75% of entries, and are used for characters that contain a clearly defined duality of only two components, allowing group two characters to not only be broken apart but also display an index of all other characters that contain a given component. Group three characters were mostly reserved for a small subset of obscure edge-case characters that contain more than two components, for example, avoiding the existence of any inaccurate or made-up splits.

LAYER 3: RENDERING ENGINE

This layer is what turns a given character's authentic SVG brush stroke vector into a physical 3D object that learners can see and touch. A custom built API was used to convert each SVG vector into a three-dimensional mesh. samplePathPoints() converts each stroke from SVG into a clean polyline. strokeToMesh() then takes the polyline and pushes the original point outward along the perpendicular by half the stroke's width in each direction creating two parallel curves that trace along both sides of the stroke. It then traces a connecting line down the left edge, then back up the right edge in reverse in order to create a full two-dimensional outline of the stroke. It then pushes the flat outline up along the z-axis, turning it into a real solid three-dimensional structure with thickness, rounded edges, and a surface that catches light.

LAYER 4: USER INTERFACE

Sitting directly on top of the render engine is the user interface itself, built entirely from one reusable translucent "glass" component. By using CSS properties like backdrop-blur, brightness and saturation, the glass boxes are able to behave visually like a physical pane of glass that can obscure the live render behind it in real time, allowing the user interface itself to take on the role as the glass structure that encases terrarium itself. The functionality of the interface is also designed to be extremely easy to use, with interactive elements that glow when hovered over, and display additional information about a given feature.

The interface also includes a radio feature that allows the user to select any track from the Duality Terrarium EP: an electronic music project created to enhance the user experience by auditorily complementing the retro-futurist design of the UI. The EP was produced using Ableton Live 12.2 and integrates chopped and looped drum breaks over compositions written and played on a Wavestation VTS, a first-of-its-kind synthesizer released in 1990 that used wave sequencing and vector synthesis to constantly fade between different digital waveforms, creating its distinctive glassy, shimmering, slowly evolving atmospheric sound.

RUNTIME ENVIRONMENT

The rendering engine and user interface execute natively in-browser over WebGL, allowing the GPU to handle the 3D renders in real time as the user breaks apart characters and manipulates them in any way they please. Any one of 6703 fully interactive three-dimensional objects are generated on demand within a single page Javascript application.
